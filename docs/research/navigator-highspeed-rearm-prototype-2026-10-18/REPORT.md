# High-speed post-unwind rearming: local prototype

The installed correction has a real coverage gap: after steering demand retreats, its unwind latch requires five confirmed samples above 102% of the preceding bend's peak. A genuinely rising new request can remain blocked simply because it never exceeds that older peak. This is a command-admission diagnosis, not proof that the block caused the owner's reported lane-edge event.

In the <private clock time> CDT fixture, the old retained peak was 0.000488624/m; the required new request exceeded 0.000498396/m. The latch first set around route time 1068.929 s, about 12 s before driver pressure. Some intervening requests rise from lower troughs, but much of the final approach is actual withdrawal. A body-over-response reading alone cannot distinguish a fresh demand from continued unwind.

## Prototype

[prototype.py](../../navigator-reference/PRIVATE_ARTIFACTS.md) is a research copy, not paired production source. It preserves the existing above-old-peak path and adds an alternative after the output has fully rejoined the reference: observe a fresh five/six-sample demand window spanning at least 200 ms, with meaningful rise both from the trough and across the current window. Current body, wheel, calibration, timing and other admission checks still apply. Normal correction persistence restarts after rearming.

A provisional new peak retains the previous peak until actual correction begins. Withdrawal or invalid evidence during onset restores the retained peak/latch. Invalid evidence also clears causal filter samples, preventing stale low demand from manufacturing an apparent rise after interruption. Lower plateaus, short pulses and continuous withdrawal do not earn a new leg. Original caps, slew, native bounds, driver withdrawal and the unchanged reference remain in effect. Thresholds are engineering hypotheses, not fitted gains.

## What remains unresolved

The [final approach timeline](../../navigator-reference/PRIVATE_ARTIFACTS.md) preserves requested curvature, wire path and raw requested/measured wheel angles for follow-up; the wheel angles are not treated as interchangeable calibrated channels.

The prototype makes **zero command changes in the final 2.8 s before the reported <private clock time> input**. Around route time 1078–1080.8 s the requested curvature magnitude largely falls from about 0.000489/m to 0.000276/m. The unwind block there is consistent with its purpose. The prototype rearms earlier around 1073 s, but replay cannot tell us how that would have changed the later physical path.

This is therefore a narrowly tested rearming prototype, not a demonstrated fix for the lane-edge/debris event. Next work should separate requested path behavior, actual wheel unwind/delay and uncertain body-yaw response during the retained event, rather than overriding the withdrawal latch or deriving a gain from response ratios. The reported object, tire crossing and controller causation remain unverified.

Paired branches and protected source stay untouched; 1.20/0.97, geometry, fixed pinion and current configuration are preserved. No special high-speed reproduction is needed or requested.
